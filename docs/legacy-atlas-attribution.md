# Historical teaching atlas

The plate images in this directory are selections from the user-supplied **New Spencerian Compendium of Penmanship**, by **P. R. Spencer's Sons**, published by Ivison, Blakeman and Company, New York and Chicago. The nineteenth-century original plates retain their original forms. Several plates carry dates from 1879 through 1886. This atlas does not reproduce a modern re-typeset edition.

Source scan: `New Spencerian Compendium_text.pdf` (175 physical PDF pages). The PDF identifies the Internet Archive as the digitizer and supplies this record: https://archive.org/details/NewSpencerianCompendium .

`atlas.json` gives the exact physical PDF page (one-based) and the printed page or plate number for every image. Physical PDF page numbers must not be treated as the printed plate numbers. For example, physical PDF page 73 is printed Plate 2.

Images were rendered from the source, converted to grayscale, corrected for uneven scan illumination, and resized for the app. Some images are identified excerpts of a larger plate. No letter outlines, stroke paths, or shade placements were redrawn. Original printing defects, faint lines and engraving artifacts remain visible. Fine details should be checked against the full measured plate and the source PDF.

The second historical source, **Spencerian Key to Practical Penmanship**, by H. C. Spencer, appears in the supplied `spencerian_penmanship.pdf` (206 physical PDF pages). It has an 1866 copyright notice and identifies the source record as https://archive.org/details/cu31924029485467 . It informs the course's historical comparison; its pages are not duplicated in this plate atlas. The Key and Compendium use different principle classifications; the app follows the Compendium's seven-principle sequence.

**Modern supplied sources are separate.** `CLASS-SpenceModule-01download.pdf` is a modern practice-sheet publication. Its cover states "for personal use only" and gives THEFOZZYBOOK.PH. No pages, illustrations, tracing templates, or practice-sheet artwork from that manual are redistributed in this app. `Best Pens, Pencils, Paper, and Writing Tools for Spencerian Script.pdf` is the user's supplied research report; the atlas contains none of its page artwork. New lesson prose and app-generated guides are separate from all source plate images.

Historical posture drawings show right-handed writers and period furniture. Their inclusion records the historical method. Learners should adapt the chair, work height, pen and paper for comfort. Historical references to gendered writing styles are source labels, not restrictions on who can use the forms.
